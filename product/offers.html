Nine ways to charge for this, and the one I'd pick.

Each block is a real offer architecture from the indie software and developer-tool space, applied to Pass with actual numbers. Conventions are cited where they exist rather than invented. Read the failure mode on each — that's the part that decides it.

01

The layered ladder

2026 default

The convention most indie Mac apps converged on: a free tier with real value, a cheap annual as the revenue driver, and a lifetime price as the anchor at roughly 3–4× the annual. The lifetime option exists mostly to make the annual look reasonable; a minority take it, and those who do fund the runway.

Pass fits this unusually well because the free tier costs you nothing to serve — the on-device model means no per-user inference bill.

02

Open core — free on npm, paid on the desktop

Dominant devtools model

Publish the capture format, the CLI and the MCP server free on npm and Homebrew. Charge for the Mac app — the part that needs a microphone, a cursor ring and a review panel, and which nobody can clone in an afternoon.

The package is the distribution, not the product. It goes where developers already look, it gets starred, it gets into agent toolchains, and every install is an advert for the GUI. This is why open core is the most common devtools model in 2026: the free layer buys reach that marketing can't.

03

Flat licence, bring your own key

Zero backend

One price, no accounts, no server, no inference bill. The user pastes their own API key or uses the on-device model. You ship a binary and a licence key and never think about tokens again.

Margin is effectively 100% and there is no cost that scales with a power user — the structural problem with every metered AI wrapper.

Price$49 once
Your cost$0 per user, forever
InfraA licence key and a download
TradeoffNo recurring revenue
Failure mode: one-time revenue stops while support doesn't. The usual fix is paid major versions — v2 is a new purchase for existing owners.

04

Credits — you resell the inference

Highest risk

You hold the API key and sell packs of passes. Removes the key-pasting friction entirely, which is real: onboarding a key is where most users drop.

But a pass with 20 crops costs about $0.02 on Haiku, and heavy users are exactly the ones who convert. You are now running a metered business with a proxy, accounts, abuse protection and a billing stack — infrastructure, not an app.

Cost to serve~$0.02 per pass with images
Pack price$9 for 500 passes (~$10 margin)
NeedsProxy, auth, metering, Stripe
MarginFalls as usage rises
Failure mode: you are charging a second time for inference the user already pays for in their coding agent. Competitors rationing hourly allowances are a symptom of this model, not a feature of it.

05

Seats — the pass becomes a team object

Highest ceiling

The single-player tool is the wedge; the business is the shared queue. A designer records a pass, it lands in a team inbox, an engineer or an agent picks it up and closes it. Now it competes with Linear and BugHerd rather than with dictation apps.

Seat pricing is the only model here where revenue grows without your costs growing in step, and where churn is organisational rather than personal.

Price$12 per seat / month
ExpansionSeats grow with the team
NeedsAccounts, sync, web app, SSO eventually
BenchmarkUnder 3% monthly churn is healthy
Failure mode: this is a different company. Sync, permissions and a web app are months of work, and you'd be entering an occupied market.

06

The pass is the advertisement

Your existing pattern

Every pass can be published as a link — the notes, the ringed crops, the page it was recorded on. You send it to a client or a contractor instead of a Loom. They see the artifact and the tool at the same time.

This is the mechanism already documented in your own funnel research: the work is simultaneously the product demo, the proof, and the ad. One act, four outputs. Here the shared pass carries a quiet footer and the recipient is, by definition, someone who receives design feedback for a living.

07

Someone else's storefront

Distribution, not pricing

List on Setapp and let a subscription bundle of 270+ Mac apps do the selling. You take a revenue share of usage rather than a price, and you inherit an audience that is already paying for Mac software.

The same logic applies to a merchant of record — Polar, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy and Gumroad all handle VAT, invoicing and licence delivery so you never register for tax in twenty jurisdictions. For a first paid product this is the difference between shipping this month and not shipping.

08

Free, with a tip jar

Reputation play

Give it away. No licence, no key, no tiers. Add a Sponsor button and ship the source. You are not trying to make money from Pass — you are trying to be the person who made Pass.

For a freelancer at your rate, one inbound engagement traced to the tool pays for the entire build. The software is a portfolio piece with a download counter, and the counter is the proof.

09

The tool is the lead magnet

Your actual business

Pass is free and unbranded as a product. What it sells is the practice behind it: a designer who builds the instrument, not just the deliverable. The download page carries one line about who made it and what he does.

Every user is a designer or a team doing UI review with AI agents — which is precisely the buyer for an AI-native design engagement. The tool qualifies the lead by existing.

Where a user actually drops

Pricing is the last decision. The stages below are where this specific product loses people, and each one has a measurable gate.

01

Arrive

From an npm install, a shared pass, or a post. The demo has to read without sound.

Gate: 8-second ring clip

02

Install

Three permissions, one of which silently breaks the hotkey. This is the real cliff.

Gate: guided permissions

03

First pass

Activation is one completed pass with a correct ring. Nothing before it counts.

Target: under 60 seconds

04

Habit

Three passes in a week. Below that, no pricing model saves it.

Gate: it beats typing

05

Pay

Triggered by a limit that hurts — export targets or history, not a pass count.

Top devtools: 7%+

The one that matters: stage 02. You have personally hit every failure in it — a permission that looks granted but isn't, a rebuild that silently revokes it, a hotkey that does nothing. No offer architecture survives an install that appears broken.
