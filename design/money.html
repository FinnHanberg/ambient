What to meter

A free cap only works if the thing being counted is something the user chooses to spend. Meter something that accrues automatically and people change how they use the product to protect their balance — which is the opposite of what a cap is for.

G

How big is the cap

Two allowances doing different jobs. One teaches the product, one keeps the door open forever.

  1. Ten briefs to learn it. A one-off allowance, not monthly. Enough to run real reviews for two or three weeks and feel the difference between talking for four minutes and writing tickets for forty. Fewer than ten and the habit never forms; far more and there is no reason to decide.
  2. Then three a month, forever. Not a trial that ends — a free tier that persists. Someone reviewing occasionally stays free and stays happy; someone reviewing weekly hits three by the second week and upgrades without being cornered. It also means uninstalling is never the cheaper option.
  3. Capture, screenshots and raw export: never counted. The meter only moves when you ask for the written brief.

Your forty-images instinct was the right size — roughly two weeks of real use — attached to the wrong noun. Ten briefs is about the same span of time, spent on something the user actually chooses.

H

What I would ship

Five of the twelve, in this order. The rest are alternatives to reach for if these stall.

  1. A2 side-by-side transform as the default review footer, shown free on every pass. It is the demo and the argument at once, and seeing it repeatedly is what builds the want.
  2. B1 two plans with Free as a genuine tier, metered on briefs written — ten to learn it, then three a month forever. Capture, screenshots and raw export never counted.
  3. F1 + F2 together — a quiet number in the review header, and a full sentence only on the last one. Then F4 at the wall, with the return date named.
  4. C2 expiry that keeps your data. The cheapest goodwill in the product and the easiest thing to get catastrophically wrong.
  5. C3 destinations as the second lever when teams arrive. Integrations cost real maintenance, so charging for them needs no justification.

Two things to hold to: nothing may interrupt a pass, ever — not a prompt, not a badge, not a meter. And the raw path stays free and unlimited forever, because a capture tool people cannot trust to give their words back is not worth paying for either.
